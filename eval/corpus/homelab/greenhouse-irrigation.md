# Greenhouse irrigation

Two raised beds and the greenhouse, watered on a timer, because doing it by
hand means doing it well for three weeks and then badly for the rest of the
summer.

## The plumbing

A 25 mm PVC spine runs from the outdoor tap along the back wall, with a tee and
a shut-off per zone. PVC because it is cheap, it glues in an afternoon, and the
whole run is shaded — sunlight is what destroys it, and a length of PVC left
exposed to a south wall goes brittle in two seasons. The section that crosses
the path is sleeved in a wider PVC conduit so a wheelbarrow cannot crack it.

Solvent weld joints, not push fit, and dry-fit the whole run before opening the
cement — the cure is instant and a misaligned tee stays misaligned. Leave one
union near the tap so the line can be broken for winter.

Drip line off each zone, 30 cm emitter spacing, one loop per bed. Emitters
clog long before pipe fails, so the filter at the tap is the part that matters:
120 mesh, cleaned when the flow drops.

## Timing

The controller runs before dawn, when pressure is highest and evaporation
lowest. Little and often is wrong for beds — it grows shallow roots. Deep and
infrequent is right: twenty minutes twice a week for the beds, daily for the
greenhouse in July because the pots dry out completely.

A cheap soil moisture probe in bed two vetoes the cycle after rain. It is the
only sensor here and it has paid for itself in tomatoes that did not split.

## Winter

Everything above freezing depth drains in November: shut the tap, open the union
and the low-point drain, blow the drip lines out with the compressor at low
pressure. A drip emitter that froze full is a drip emitter that dribbles for the
rest of its life.

## Notes for next year

- The greenhouse zone wants its own pressure reducer; drip emitters are rated
  for far less than mains pressure and two of them blew off the line.
- Mulch after the drip line goes down, never before.
- The basil that goes into the ferment crocks wants the wettest corner, see
  [[fermentation]].
