# Espresso

Machine: dual boiler, E61 group. Grinder: 64mm flat burr, stepless. The grinder
matters more than the machine and I resented learning that.

## The dialing-in loop

Start from a recipe and change one thing at a time.

- Dose 18g in a standard basket.
- Yield 36g out (a 1:2 ratio).
- Time 25-30 seconds from the pump starting.
- Water 93C for medium roasts, 90-91C for light, 88-90C for dark.

If it runs fast, grind finer. If it chokes, coarser. Adjust the grinder, throw
away two shots to clear the old grounds from the burr chamber, then judge. Do
not change the dose and the grind in the same shot; you learn nothing.

## Taste, not numbers

- **Sour, thin, salty, empty finish** — under-extracted. Grind finer, raise the
  temperature, or extend the ratio to 1:2.5.
- **Bitter, drying, ashy, hollow middle** — over-extracted. Coarser, cooler,
  shorter.
- **Sour *and* bitter at once** — channelling. The water found a path through
  the puck and ran past the rest of it, so parts are over-extracted and parts
  were barely touched. This is a preparation problem, not a grind problem.

Channelling is the main enemy and the fix is puck preparation: break up clumps
(a WDT tool, a few pins in a cork, thirty seconds of stirring), distribute
level, tamp flat and firm — flat matters, hard does not. A puck screen keeps the
shower screen clean and marginally helps the pre-infusion soak.

## Pre-infusion

Low pressure for 5-8 seconds before the full nine bars lets the puck saturate
evenly and resist channelling. On an E61 the lever's first position does this
mechanically. Light roasts benefit most.

## Freshness

Rest beans 7-14 days after roast. Fresher than that and the CO2 makes the flow
erratic and the crema pointlessly enormous. Past about five weeks it goes flat
and papery.

Store in an airtight jar at room temperature, away from light. Not the fridge —
condensation on cold beans is worse than the oxidation you are avoiding. The
freezer genuinely works for long-term storage in sealed single-dose portions,
ground straight from frozen, but not for the bag you are drinking this week.

## Water

Espresso is 98% water and mine is very hard. Untreated, that scales the boiler
within a year and tastes flat because the high alkalinity buffers away the
acidity that makes a light roast interesting.

Third Wave Water sachets in distilled, or a home recipe of distilled plus
measured Epsom salt and sodium bicarbonate. Target roughly 70ppm total hardness
and 40ppm alkalinity. Never pure distilled or reverse osmosis alone — the
machine needs conductivity for its water level probe, and unbuffered water is
corrosive.

## Milk

Steam to 60-65C. Above 70C the lactose starts tasting scalded and the foam
collapses. Introduce air for the first two seconds only — that is the hiss —
then submerge the tip and spin the whole pitcher into a whirlpool until it is
too hot to hold at the base. Tap, swirl, pour.

Whole milk foams most forgivingly. Oat needs a barista formulation with added
buffers or it splits against the coffee's acidity.

## Maintenance

Backflush with water after every session and with detergent weekly. Descale
annually, or never if the water is properly treated. Change the grinder burrs
around 500kg, which at home means never. Clean the grinder chute monthly; stale
grounds go rancid and they taste exactly like that.
