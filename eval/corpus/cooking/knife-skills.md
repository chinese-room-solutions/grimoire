# Knives

## The three that matter

A 210mm gyuto does 90% of everything. A small paring knife for the in-hand work
a board cannot do. A serrated bread knife, because bread destroys an edge. Every
other knife in the block is optional, and the block itself is a bacteria hotel —
a magnetic strip or an in-drawer holder is better.

Blade steel is a trade between edge retention, toughness, and how easily you can
put an edge back on. A soft German stainless at 56 HRC dulls quickly and takes a
new edge on a steel in ten seconds. A Japanese white steel at 63 HRC holds
frighteningly long, chips if you hit a bone, and rusts if you leave it wet. For
a home kitchen, something in the middle (VG-10, AUS-10, SG2) is the sane
default.

## Sharpening

Dull knives cause the injuries. A sharp blade goes where you point it; a dull
one skates off an onion skin into a knuckle.

Whetstones, in order: 1000 grit to set the edge, 4000 to refine. A 400 only when
repairing a chip. Soak the stone, hold about 15 degrees per side — two stacked
coins under the spine is close enough — and work until a burr forms along the
entire length on one side. The burr is the signal that you have actually reached
the apex; without it you are polishing the shoulder and wondering why nothing
improved. Flip, raise the burr the other way, then alternate light passes to
strip it.

A honing steel does not sharpen. It straightens an edge that has rolled, which
is most of what "dull" means day to day. Use it often, sharpen it monthly.

Pull-through carbide sharpeners scrape metal away and leave a ragged edge. They
work, in the sense that a cheese grater works.

## Technique

- **Grip.** Pinch the blade between thumb and forefinger just ahead of the
  handle. Handle-only grips give up all control of the tip.
- **Guiding hand.** Claw. Fingertips curled back, knuckles against the flat of
  the blade, and the knife never rises above the knuckle. The knuckle sets the
  slice thickness.
- **Motion.** Forward and down, or back and down. Chopping straight down
  crushes; the cut happens along the length of the edge.
- **The tip stays on the board** for most work, and the heel lifts. That pivot is
  what makes fast rocking safe.

## Board

End grain wood or a soft polyethylene board. Glass, stone, and bamboo all
destroy an edge — bamboo is a grass, it is full of silica, and it is marketed as
gentle. Damp cloth underneath so it does not slide.

## Care

Hand wash, dry immediately. The dishwasher's heat and detergent ruin the handle
and knock the edge against everything else in the rack. Never leave a knife in
the sink under water, for your own hands' sake and the blade's.

Cut on the board, not in the air. Scrape with the spine, never the edge — the
scoop-with-the-blade habit is the single most common way people dull a knife
they just sharpened.
